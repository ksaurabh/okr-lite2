1. What is an OKR?

An OKR (Objective and Key Result) pairs a qualitative goal — the Objective ("what we want to achieve") — with measurable signals of success — the Key Results ("how we'll know we got there"). In this app both live as Objectives in a single tree: any objective can be flagged as a Key Result, and objectives nest under one another to form company → team → individual goals.

The golden rule: objectives are ambitious and directional; key results are specific and measurable. If you can't tell whether you hit it, it isn't a key result yet.

2. Core concepts

TermWhat it means here
ObjectiveAny goal in the tree. Has a level, type, owner, assignee, period, points, status, and can be a Key Result.
LevelCompany, Team, or Individual — who the objective belongs to.
TypeThe size/shape of the work: Initiative → Saga → Epic → Story → Subtask.
Key Result (KR)A flag on an objective marking it as a measurable result rather than a goal.
PeriodThe time box: a quarter, month, week, or a one-off date range.
Value Points (VP)The business value an item carries — used to prioritize and to check that a parent's value adds up across its children.
Story Points (SP)The effort/size estimate, used for capacity planning.
PlanA curated, ordered list of objectives for one owner and one period. (See section 6.)

3. Creating objectives

Go to the Objectives page and add an objective. The editor has two tabs:

Fields tab

  • Title (always visible) & Description
  • Level and Period (both required)
  • Parent objective — nest this under a bigger goal
  • Team, Owner, and Assignee
  • Story Points and Value Points
  • Resolved date, a Link, and Tags
  • Private (only you) and Read-only (only owner/assignee can add children) toggles
  • Progress Updates and full History of changes

Comments tab

Anyone on your team can leave a comment. Comments are listed newest-first and show who wrote them and when — handy for discussion that isn't a formal progress update.

Mark an objective as a Key Result (the KR field) to distinguish your measurable outcomes from the broader objectives they support.

4. Building a hierarchy

OKRs are most powerful when they ladder up. Set a parent objective so individual work rolls into team goals, and team goals into company goals:

1
Create a Company-level Initiative (e.g. "Become the category leader").
2
Add Team-level objectives beneath it (Epics/Sagas) owned by each team.
3
Break those into Individual Stories and Subtasks with owners and assignees.

On the Objectives page you can filter by period, team, owner, assignee, level, type, status, and tags, choose which columns to show, resize them, and save the result as a View for one-click reuse later.

5. Periods & tags

Use the Periods page to define your time boxes — quarters, months, weeks, or one-off ranges — and nest them (e.g. weeks inside a quarter). Every objective and plan is anchored to a period, which is what makes "this quarter's OKRs" a meaningful filter.

Use the Tags page to create colored labels (themes, workstreams, initiatives) and apply several to any objective for cross-cutting filtering.

6. Plans

A Plan is a focused, hand-ordered selection of objectives for one owner in one period. While the Objectives tree is the full picture, a plan is "what this person is actually committing to this quarter."

What a plan holds

FieldPurpose
Name & colorIdentify the plan
Owner & PeriodRequired — these are what turn a list into a plan
LevelCompany / Team / Individual
StatusThe plan's stage (configurable; defaults: New → In Review → In Execution → In Retrospective → Closed → Archived)
Parent planPlans can have child plans, which power the split view
ItemsThe objectives in the plan, kept in your chosen order
HistoryEvery add/remove/move, with who and when

The Plans overview lists every plan with its owner, period, level and status, and lets you filter by status (multi-select, defaulting to "All but archived"). Add objectives to a plan from the Objectives page using the bookmark icon, then drag items to reorder them.

7. The split plan view

Open a plan to see it side by side: the parent/current plan on the left, and a selected child plan plus its objective tree on the right. This is where roll-up planning happens — the parent commits the value, the children deliver it.

  • Click any item in the parent plan to filter the child plan to that item's children.
  • When an item is selected, its card shows Children VP — the value its children add up to.
  • Use the three-dots menu (in the plan header) to select a child plan, create or attach child plans, edit which columns show, toggle the objective tree, and view history.
  • Every column shown on a card is editable inline via quick-edit.
Green dot: the item's VP matches the sum of its children's VP.   Orange dot: it doesn't add up (or the item has VP but no children in the child plan) — a signal to revisit your breakdown.

8. Value points & roll-ups

Value Points let you sanity-check a plan: a parent objective's VP should equal the total VP of the children that deliver it. The split view surfaces mismatches with the green/orange dots above, and shows Total VP per plan so you can see where value concentrates. Story Points play the same role for effort, feeding capacity planning on the Teams page.

9. The OKR Agent

The Agent page is a guided, number-driven assistant. Just type the number of the option you want; every prompt offers Go back and Start over, and each question carries a short code (e.g. [Q-7DD0]) so you can reference it.

Set my OKRs

Pick a user, a duration type (quarterly/monthly/weekly), then a specific duration — the agent shows how many plans match at each step (with an open/closed breakdown) and then lists the plan's items, ordered by value points. From there you can update VP item-by-item, drill into an item's parent/siblings/children, change an item's duration, and view a child plan side by side.

Side-by-side & breakdowns

After choosing a child plan to view side by side, a deeper menu lets you show a breakdown by assignee (item count and VP sum per person) or list the unassigned items in the child plan.

Browse Objective Tree

Explore top-level initiatives (optionally with open children), your overdue-but-open objectives, or group results by duration — all rendered with the same objective tree as the main page, with per-question column memory. Chat sessions are saved and can be resumed, archived, or deleted.

10. Tracking progress

  • Workflow status on each objective moves work along: To Do → In Backlog → In Planning → In Progress → In Acceptance → Done (and Archived).
  • Progress updates and comments capture the narrative; history captures every field change automatically.
  • The Dashboard, Progress, Weekly Updates and Updates pages roll this up so you can review status at a glance.
  • Log Work records time spent against your work.

11. Teams & capacity

On the Teams page, define teams (including a special Self / individual contributor type) and record Team Assignments — who is on a team, at what capacity (story points per week), and for what date range. The Capacity Assigned by Individual section then shows, as of any date, how loaded each person is against a 40-point week, so you can plan realistically.

12. Settings & admin

  • Plan stages — admins define the list of statuses a plan can move through (the New → … → Archived progression).
  • Jira integration — connect objectives to Jira epics; export/import the integration settings as JSON.
  • Admins can also manage the organization, and super-admins can impersonate a user to see exactly what they see.

13. A suggested workflow

1
Define the period (e.g. this quarter) on the Periods page.
2
Set company objectives and key results at the top level.
3
Cascade team and individual objectives beneath them, assigning owners, value points, and effort.
4
Build plans per owner for the period; use child plans + the split view to verify value rolls up (watch for orange dots).
5
Execute & track: advance workflow status, post progress updates and comments, and review on the Dashboard.
6
Close out: move plans to In Retrospective → Closed, reflect, and roll learnings into the next period.
